# free-refill-cost

**Topic:** What's the real cost of a free refill?
**Kind:** advice
**Hook:** custom (`hook.js`): a coffee pot keeps topping up Leo's mug; every refill pops the register drawer and a coin flies out, while a FREE REFILLS tally ticks up
**Background:** butter (pad: mellow)
**Voice:** speech, Roma Tom narrates
**Length:** about 33 s

The idea: a "free" refill still costs coffee, milk and sugar. Small per cup, real over a year.
Refills are still worth keeping because they bring guests back, but choose the policy on purpose:
one refill for dine-in, or a small charge, and know your cost per cup.

## Fact notes

All money figures are **example numbers for a typical independent cafe**, framed with "Say…".

- **Coffee, 50¢ a refill:** a 12 oz drip refill (about 355 ml) brewed at about 60 g of coffee per
  liter uses about 21 g of beans. Wholesale specialty beans at about $11 a pound (454 g) = 2.4¢
  a gram; × 21 g ≈ **$0.51**, rounded to 50¢.
- **Milk and sugar, 10¢:** a splash of milk or creamer and a sugar packet (example).
- **Per refill:** 0.50 + 0.10 = **60¢**, sold for $0.
- **Year:** 0.60 × 25 refills a day × 360 open days = **$5,400**.
- **Scale:** an illustration of the trade-off, not data. No claim about how many guests return.
- **Fix:** common policies: free refills for dine-in only, a limit of one, or a small refill
  price (for example $1).
