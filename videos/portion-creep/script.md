## Fact notes

- **The chicken bowl, $14, $3.50 recipe food cost, 4 oz recipe, 5 oz served:** example figures
  for a made-up cafe, framed on screen and in the voice with "Say your chicken bowl…". $3.50 on
  $14 is a 25% plate cost, inside the usual 25 to 35% food cost range owners aim for.
- **"That extra ounce costs a quarter":** example price of $4.00 a pound for boneless chicken
  breast, which is $0.25 an ounce (4.00 / 16). US retail boneless breast has run roughly $4 a
  pound (USDA ERS / BLS average retail prices, 2024 to 2026); wholesale is often a little less,
  so a quarter is a fair round example.
- **$10,800 a year:** worked calculation: $0.25 × 120 bowls a day × 360 open days = $10,800.
  120 bowls a day and 360 days are example figures.
- **Dev's line:** a character's line, not a statistic. Eyeballing instead of using a recipe
  and a measured scoop is the usual cause of portion creep.
- **The fix:** standard portion-control practice (scales at the line, sized portioning scoops
  or dishers, plate photos at the pass); a practical recommendation, not a statistic.

## Post caption

One extra ounce of chicken costs about a quarter. Nobody notices a quarter. But on 120 bowls a day, all year, it comes to $10,800. Portion creep isn't anyone being careless: it's scooping until it looks right. Weigh one plate every shift, give the line portion scoops, and pin a photo of each dish at the pass.
