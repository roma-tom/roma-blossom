# black-friday

**Question:** "Should I run 30% off on Black Friday?"
**Background:** lime
**Length:** 39.6 s

The idea: a big storewide discount fills the room but guts what you keep per guest, and the
deal hunters it brings often leave for the next deal. Keep full price and spend a little on a
reason to come back instead.

## Fact notes

All figures are **example costs for a typical independent cafe**, not data from a real business.
They are there to show the method.

- **Lunch, $15:** food cost $4.50 = 30% of the price, a common food-cost target for cafes.
  Kept at full price: 15.00 − 4.50 = **$10.50**.
- **30% off:** 0.30 × 15.00 = $4.50 off. Kept: 15.00 − 4.50 − 4.50 = **$6.00**.
- **To match a normal day:** 100 lunches × $10.50 = $1,050. At $6.00 a lunch:
  1,050 ÷ 6.00 = **175 lunches**, 75% more covers (and more staff time) for the same money.
- **Myth scene:** no statistic is claimed. "Often" reflects general marketing practice: guests
  won by a deep discount are price-driven and tend to compare deals, so they're less likely to
  return at full price than guests who came for the food or the place.
- **Versus:** the same guest, two ways. 30% off today keeps $6.00 for one visit. Full price
  today keeps $10.50; a December visit with a free side (costing you about $1.50 to make) keeps
  10.50 − 1.50 = $9.00; together 10.50 + 9.00 = **$19.50** over two visits. It assumes the
  bounce-back card brings the guest back once; if they don't come back, you still kept $10.50
  instead of $6.00 on the first visit.
- "Kept" counts ingredients only (gross profit per lunch). Labour, rent and card fees come out of
  it too, which makes the discount's bite even bigger.
- **Fix steps:** general practice for bounce-back offers: a small reward redeemable on a later
  visit within a set window, and tracking redemptions to see how many guests return.

## Post caption

30% off packs the room on Black Friday. But a full room isn't the same as a full till. Keep
your prices and give guests a reason to come back in December instead.
