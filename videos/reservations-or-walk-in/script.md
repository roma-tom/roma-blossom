# reservations-or-walk-in

**Question:** "Should you take reservations, or stay walk-in only?"
**Background:** paper (pad: cosy)
**Length:** about 20 s
**Voice:** babble. The speech voice model couldn't be downloaded in the session that made this
video (the session's network allowlist denies huggingface.co). The `say` lines are written for
speech: delete the `voice` line in `storyboard.json` and render again to get Roma Tom speaking.

The idea: it isn't one or the other. Reservations help you plan staff and prep; walk-in only is
simple and has no no-shows. Most places do best mixing both, and a quick cafe can stay walk-in.

## Fact notes

No numbers or statistics are shown or spoken.

- **Reservations help planning:** knowing covers in advance lets you schedule staff and size
  prep for the night (general operating practice, not a statistic).
- **Walk-in only has no no-shows:** by definition, no bookings means no booked tables left
  empty.
- **Maria's quote:** a character's view, not a statistic.
- **Fix:** a hybrid (bookings for large parties and peak nights, some tables held for walk-ins)
  is a common practice in independent restaurants; quick-service cafes typically don't need
  bookings. Advice, not a statistic.
