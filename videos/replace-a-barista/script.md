# replace-a-barista

**Question:** "What does it really cost to replace one barista?"
**Background:** blush (pad: drift)
**Length:** about 25 s
**Voice:** babble. The speech voice model couldn't be downloaded in the session that made this
video (huggingface.co was blocked). The `say` lines are written for speech: delete the `voice`
line in `storyboard.json` and render again to get Roma Tom speaking them.

The idea: when a barista quits, the cost isn't just the job ad. Training pay, the trainer's time,
remakes and a slower line add up to about $2,000. Lose three a year and that's $6,000. Spending a
bit of that on keeping people is cheaper than replacing them.

## Fact notes

All money figures are **example numbers for a small independent cafe**, framed in the voice
with "Say one barista quits…". Real costs vary with wages, region and how hiring is done.

- **Job ad, $150:** a sponsored listing on a job board for a week or two (example).
- **Interviews, $150:** about 5 hours of the owner's or manager's time at $30 an hour (example).
- **Training pay, $640:** a week of training shifts, 40 hours × $16 an hour, while the new hire
  is not yet working at full speed (example).
- **Trainer's time, $360:** about 20 hours of a senior barista's time at $18 an hour spent
  training instead of serving (example).
- **Remakes, waste, $200:** spilled milk, remade drinks and wasted shots in the first month
  (example).
- **Slower line, $500:** lost sales while the new hire is slow, guests walking out of a longer
  line during the first weeks (example).
- **Total:** 150 + 150 + 640 + 360 + 200 + 500 = **$2,000**.
- **Three a year:** $2,000 × 3 = **$6,000** (example; three departures in a year is an example
  for a team of about eight).
- **$1 raise:** $1 × 25 hours a week × 52 weeks = **$1,300 a year**, less than the $2,000 to
  replace that barista.
- **Context (not shown):** research on hospitality turnover finds the cost of losing one hourly
  employee runs into the thousands of dollars once hiring, training and lost productivity are
  counted. Tracey, J. B. and Hinkin, T. R., "The Costs of Employee Turnover", Cornell Center for
  Hospitality Research (2006),
  https://ecommons.cornell.edu/bitstream/handle/1813/71741/Hinkin23_The_cost_of_turnover.pdf
  The video uses its own worked example rather than that study's figure.
