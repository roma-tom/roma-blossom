# happy-hour-math

**Topic:** Is your happy hour making or losing money?
**Kind:** advice
**Hook type:** hook-rule (note "Happy hour fills the room", stamped AT A LOSS?)
**Background:** lime (pad: mellow)
**Voice:** speech, Roma Tom narrates

The idea: a happy hour glass keeps a fraction of what a full-price glass does, so it only pays
when it fills hours that would otherwise be empty and brings food orders with it.

## Fact notes

All money figures are **example numbers**, framed in the voice with "Say a glass of house wine
is nine dollars…".

- **The wine, $2.25 a glass:** a bottle of house wine bought at $11.25, poured into five
  five-ounce glasses: 11.25 ÷ 5 = **$2.25** (example). A standard bottle is 750 ml, about 25
  ounces, so five 5-ounce pours.
- **Labor, $1.25 a glass:** a bartender at about $20 an hour with tips counted separately,
  pouring about 16 glasses an hour among other work: 20 ÷ 16 = **$1.25** (example).
- **Card fee:** 3% of the price (example rate). Happy hour: 3% × $5 = **$0.15**. Full price:
  3% × $9 = **$0.27**.
- **Happy hour, you keep:** 5.00 − 2.25 − 1.25 − 0.15 = **$1.35**.
- **Full price, you keep:** 9.00 − 2.25 − 1.25 − 0.27 = **$5.23**.
- **Almost four glasses:** 5.23 ÷ 1.35 = **3.87**.
- **Fix:** running discounts in otherwise empty hours and pairing them with food are common
  operating advice, not statistics.
