## Fact notes

- **Example figures** (said as "Say…"): a $50 card redeemed in January, with food cost and
  staff time of $15 each (30% food, 30% labor, an example split). Net on that day: $0 new cash,
  $30 of cost, shown as −$30.
- **"A promise", not income:** in accounting, a gift card sale is recorded as a liability
  (deferred revenue) until the card is redeemed. Wording kept plain, no tax advice.
- **"Can't expire for at least five years":** the Credit CARD Act of 2009 and Regulation E
  (12 CFR 1005.20): gift card funds can't expire until at least five years after the card was
  issued or last loaded. Federal Reserve announcement:
  https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/bcreg20100323a.htm ; CFPB:
  https://www.consumerfinance.gov/archive/newsroom/consumer-financial-protection-bureau-seeks-input-on-gift-card-laws/
  Some states go further (some ban expiry entirely); the line says "at least".

## Post caption

A gift card sale and income sound like the same thing. They're not. Until the card is spent, you still owe the coffee, the food and the staff time. Keep gift card money aside, track what's still owed, and remember federal law says the money can't expire for at least five years.
