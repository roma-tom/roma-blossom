# coupon-worth-it

**Question:** "Is a 10% off coupon worth it?"
**Background:** paper
**Length:** 22.4 s

The idea: 10% off the price is a much bigger slice of what you actually keep. A coupon only pays
if it brings extra guests, ideally new ones in slow hours who come back at full price.

## Fact notes

All figures are **example numbers for a typical independent cafe**, not data from a real
business. The post caption says so.

- **Order, $12:** a coffee and a pastry. Ingredient cost $3.60 = 30% of the price, a common
  food-and-drink cost target for cafes. Kept per order without coupon: 12.00 − 3.60 = **$8.40**.
- **Coupon:** 10% × $12 = **$1.20**. Kept with coupon: 8.40 − 1.20 = **$7.20**.
- **"1/7 of your profit":** 1.20 ÷ 8.40 = 14.3% ≈ 1/7. ("Profit" here means gross profit after
  ingredients; labour, rent and card fees come out of it too, which makes the coupon's share even bigger.)
- **Break even:** 100 orders × $8.40 = $840. At $7.20 per order: 840 ÷ 7.20 = 116.7 → **117 orders**,
  i.e. about **17% more** orders just to keep the same money.
- **Regulars scene:** illustrative only (3 of 6 is not a statistic). The point is the logic: a
  coupon used by guests who would have come anyway is pure lost margin; it only pays when it
  brings guests who return at full price.
- **Fix steps:** general practice: target discounts at off-peak times and new customers, and
  track redemptions and repeat visits so you know if it worked.
