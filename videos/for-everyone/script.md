# for-everyone

**Question:** "Should your cafe try to be for everyone?"
**Background:** blush (pad: sunny)
**Length:** about 31 s (no hook scene: it opens on the street; the cover carries the question)

The idea: a sign that tries to speak to everyone doesn't give anyone walking by a reason to
stop. A sign that names one guest and their problem (office workers with a short lunch break)
tells those people "this is for you", so they turn in, and come back. Pick the guest you serve
best, say it on the sign, and build the menu for them.

## Fact notes

- **The two cafes and their counts** (0, 1, 1, 2, 2 versus 2, 5, 9, 14, 20 guests a day,
  running totals) are a **made-up example**, framed in the voice with "Picture two cafes…".
  Both lanes show the same number of people walking by each day (five), so the only thing
  that changes is the sign. No statistic is claimed.
- **"Lunch in 10 minutes":** an example of a sign that names a guest (office workers) and the
  problem they have (a short break). Any concrete promise to a clear guest works the same way.
- **The myth:** the general marketing idea of positioning and target customers: a clear
  promise to one group makes you easier to choose for that group. Presented as advice, not a
  number.
- **The fix:** a practical how-to, no figures.
